# Meaning before decoration

The original collection mixed identity, editorial metaphors and atmosphere. MV Symbiosis has a direct relationship to the department mark. Common Ground has a clear real/virtual exchange metaphor. The orbits, pale arches and bronze leaves mostly established a mood; they did not by themselves explain a particular research idea.

The revised collection gives the motion a relationship to express. It retains the original eight stages and Astra looks, and adds separate Meaning A/B compositions with editable relational sculptures. This keeps the earlier designs available for comparison.

## Research basis

The [official logo explanation](https://meta.kaist.ac.kr/logo) assigns blue to challenge, creativity and science/technology, and red to a warm, inclusive, human-centered society. Its fused MV expresses a future in which people live together across spatial and temporal boundaries. **Red as physical and blue as virtual is this production's editorial convention, not the official color definition.**

The [faculty and labs directory](https://meta.kaist.ac.kr/labs/) identifies work on human–space interaction and spatial design, avatar telepresence, human–computer interaction, digital twins, remote collaboration, and social networks. These themes give us more specific subjects than generic futuristic sculpture.

[UVR's research descriptions](https://uvrlab.org/research) connect Meta-Objects to counterparts that share form, properties and function across physical and virtual settings, including interaction in both directions. They also describe shared XR spaces and social co-presence. A [department research feature](https://meta.kaist.ac.kr/board/post/2378/) discusses connecting spatial experiences and sharing or transferring experience and knowledge.

These sources inform the design. They do not officially endorse these artworks, and the abstract animations are not simulations or visualizations of measured research data.

## What changes on screen

| Stage | Meaning | Visible relationship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blue Hour | Co-presence | Two distinct presences move toward one shared table and turn toward a common activity. |
| Common Ground | Co-creation | Complementary parts jointly form a pavilion, hold the result and reopen. |
| Soft Architecture | Shared coordinates | A solid spatial frame and a virtual frame register into one opening. |
| Living Research | Mutual response | A signal and its reply connect two matching surfaces; both respond. |
| Shared Reality | A place to meet | Shared spatial coordinates on the front wall pair with co-presence on the tiled wall. |
| MV Symbiosis | People and technology in harmony | The actual six logo facets change form, find the MV arrangement and unfold again. |
| Serein Atelier | A commons made together | Complementary parts construct a shared pavilion inside a human-scale room. |
| MV Light Court | A responsive counterpart | Matching physical/virtual forms show an action and a return response. |

The pavilion is an editorial metaphor for joint creation; the paired frames represent registration; the presences suggest people without pretending to be tracked avatars. Reusing a mechanism in different stages is intentional: the sculpture carries the research relationship, while the room or palette sets the tone.

Each composition keeps the central seated/standing interview zone quiet on the floor-mounted4.44×2.5m wall. The explanation and source links belong in the editor, not on the LED wall. Slow absolute-time motion, group editing, independent clocks and optional video remain available.
